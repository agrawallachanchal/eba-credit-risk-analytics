Method note · bank capital · October 2026

Could EU banks absorb the ECB's 2025 NPE shock from the capital they hold today?

A bottom-up test of CET1 headroom for ten large euro-area banks, built on the EBA's 2025 Transparency Exercise and the ECB's adverse non-performing-exposure path.

Chanchal Agrawalla · Data as of 30 June 2025 · Educational analysis of public data, not investment advice

Summary

  • The ten core banks start with a median of 3.8 pp of CET1 above their own requirement (the level that triggers restrictions on distributions).
  • Charging the ECB's 3-year adverse NPE shock (+4.1 pp of gross loans) against today's capital cuts the median to 0.5 pp. BAWAG, ING and BNP Paribas fall below their requirement, by 1.4, 1.0 and 0.3 pp.
  • If banks keep earning at the June 2025 run rate, none breaches. The result therefore depends on profitability surviving the downturn, which is exactly what the ECB adverse scenario puts under pressure.
  • Confidence is medium. The model stresses credit losses only, applies one system-wide shock, and holds revenues and risk-weighted assets constant. Each limitation and the direction of its bias is listed below.

1. The question, and why headroom is the right output

Rising non-performing exposures are the main route by which a downturn reaches bank capital. In the ECB's 2025 adverse scenario, loan losses removed 5.0 pp of CET1, the largest single driver of a 4.0 pp net depletion. When a bank's CET1 ratio falls below its requirement, it faces automatic restrictions on dividends, AT1 coupons and variable pay (CRD Article 141, the maximum distributable amount or MDA mechanism).

The distance to that trigger is what matters to shareholders, AT1 holders and supervisors. The model's output is therefore headroom, the stressed CET1 ratio minus the bank's own requirement, not the stressed ratio itself. The two can rank banks differently: requirements in the sample range from 9.6% to 12.2%.

2. Data

The source is the EBA EU-wide Transparency Exercise 2025: 119 banks from 25 home countries, plus an EBA "All other banks" aggregate, reported at four quarter-ends. The analysis uses 30 June 2025. The data are bank-level and item-level with common supervisory definitions, which makes them reproducible and comparable across banks. Annual reports would not be.

The NPE ratio is the EBA's supervisory measure, which includes exposures "unlikely to pay" regardless of days past due. It therefore runs higher than banks' own NPL ratios (Santander 3.35% on this basis against 2.92% reported; BNP Paribas 2.79% against 1.6%). Because the ECB's NPE path uses the same definition, shock and base are like for like.

4. The model

Chain of calculation

For each bank, the NPE shock is applied to total gross loans (performing plus non-performing), because the shock moves the NPE ratio of the whole book. The new NPE are provisioned at the bank's own current coverage ratio.

ΔNPE = shock × gross loans
Provisions = ΔNPE × current NPE coverage
Stressed CET1 = CET1 − provisions
Headroom = stressed CET1 ÷ RWA − CET1 requirement

RWA is held constant and the balance sheet is static, as in the EBA and ECB methodology. This isolates the credit-loss channel.

Only the adverse end point is stated in the ECB text. The other paths were read by measuring the plotted lines against the chart axis, calibrated to the two published values. Reading the adverse line at end-2027 gives 5.74% against the published 5.8%, a calibration error of about 0.06 pp.

The requirement

Each bank's own published CET1 requirement is used: 4.5% Pillar 1, plus the CET1 part of the Pillar 2 requirement (at least 56.25% of it), plus the combined buffer requirement. Pillar 2 Guidance is excluded because breaching it does not trigger the MDA. Figures come from SREP announcements or Pillar 3 template EU KM1.

Sample

Ten core banks span a wide range of size, NPE ratio and starting CET1 ratio. Two deliberate contrast cases sit alongside. Kommuninvest lends only to Swedish municipalities and has no NPE; a commercial-loan shock applied to it would erase about 80% of its CET1, an artefact, so it is excluded from the stress run. Revolut is stressed but reported separately and kept out of medians. Morgan Stanley Europe stays in despite a 0.02% NPE ratio: the exclusion of Kommuninvest rests on what the bank is, not on its current numbers, and dropping healthy banks would bias the sample toward weaker ones.

Three observations. First, the three banks that breach are the three with the lowest starting headroom (2.2 to 2.4 pp). Second, the provisions cost the core banks between 1.5 and 4.2 pp of RWA, so starting headroom, not the size of the loss alone, decides the outcome. Third, a one-year adverse shock (+1.2 pp) is absorbed by all ten banks from existing capital.

6. Why the headline ignores earnings

The model stresses credit losses but not revenue. Crediting three years of profit at the June 2025 run rate would add 4 to 14 pp of CET1 per bank, more than the 1.5 to 4.2 pp the shock removes. That would show capital rising under stress, contradicting the ECB's 4.0 pp net fall, in which the adverse scenario cuts net interest income capacity by 2.6 pp and fee and dividend income by 1.0 pp against baseline.

The headline therefore asks a narrower and more robust question: can today's capital absorb the shock? The earnings view, using the EBA's own 30% tax rate and 30% payout floor (zero in loss years), is shown beside it and labelled optimistic. That no bank breaches there says the result depends on continued profitability, not that the risk is absent.

7. Sensitivity and benchmark

  • Shock ±0.3 pp: the same three banks breach.
  • Coverage on new NPE +10 pp: breaches rise from three to six of the ten core banks. This is the most important sensitivity: in a downturn, collateral values fall and coverage on new defaults is typically higher than on the stock.
  • Payout 0% and 50% (earnings view): tested in the model workbook.
  • Against the ECB: the model's 1.5 to 4.2 pp credit cost sits below the ECB's 5.0 pp loan-loss contribution. That is expected. The model provisions new defaults at current coverage, while the ECB's perimeter also covers impairments on performing loans.

This is not a forecast and not a replication of the supervisory stress test. It does not cover market, operational or funding risk.

9. Checks, and how to reproduce it

Every model input reconciles to the source: CET1 divided by RWA reproduces the reported ratio for each bank, and four banks' ratios match their Pillar 3 reports at 30 June 2025. The workbook (1,390 formulas, no errors) was recomputed independently outside Excel and agrees to within 10−15. All 40 analytical queries run unchanged on PostgreSQL 16 and in the browser on DuckDB-WASM.
